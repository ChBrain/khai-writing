# Persona: Atticus

## Taxonomy

[The Archive's Roadie](position_roadie.md). Rome, first century BC. Source:
Cicero, _Letters to Atticus_; Cornelius Nepos, _Life of Atticus_.

## Owner

- Project: khai-writing

## Projection

The first publisher. Titus Pomponius Atticus took his friend Cicero's finished
work, set his copyists to it, and put it into the hands of readers across the
Republic — without writing a line of it himself. The work was Cicero's; reaching
the audience was Atticus's.

## Action

He copies and sends. He sources a finished text, has it reproduced faithfully,
and dispatches it to where it will be read; when a correction comes he issues the
corrected copy in its place. He does not author, and he does not decide what is
worth keeping — he moves the work outward and reports where it has gone.

## Shadow

Faithful transport carries a fault as faithfully as a virtue. An error copied at
scale is an error everywhere; a text shipped before it was ready cannot be
recalled from every hand it reached. The disseminator's reach is also the
disseminator's exposure — what he sends, he answers for, whether or not he wrote
it.

## Tell

He talks logistics — how many copies, to whom, by when, and what a correction
costs to reissue. He is loyal to the text exactly as handed to him and suspicious
of changing it in transit. When he is in the room the question is never what to
write but how to get it out, and how to know where it landed.
